01 — THE RANGE

Focus Pad Types, Family by Family

Focus pads are not one product — they are a family, and each family trains a different skill. A mitt that is perfect for punch combinations is the wrong tool for full-power kicks, and a buyer who orders the wrong family ends up with returns. These are the families we manufacture, what each one is actually for, and who buys it — all producible in leather or synthetic, in your colours, under your label. The full training range around them sits on our boxing equipment manufacturer overview.

T1 — CURVED FOCUS MITTS

Curved Focus Mitts

The coach's standard: a curved striking face that catches punches cleanly and gives fast, precise feedback on every combination. The volume line for boxing gyms, fight clubs and coaching ranges — the mitt a coach holds for hours, so the grip, the wrist support and a foam build that protects the coach's hand matter as much as the face.

T2 — FLAT FOCUS PADS

Flat Focus Pads

A flat striking face for speed work, accuracy drills and sharp combination calling — the pad for footwork, feints and fast hands rather than power. A strong retail family: it is the natural second pad in a coach's kit and the entry model in many gym ranges, light enough to hold through long sessions.

T3 — THAI PADS

Thai Pads

Built for the other half of striking: kicks, knees and elbows. A longer, thicker pad held on the forearm with straps, so the holder can take repeated roundhouse kicks and knees in Muay Thai and kickboxing sessions. The essential pad for any gym that teaches striking beyond punches — and the family most buyers order alongside their mitts.

T4 — KICK SHIELDS

Kick Shields

The biggest pad in the family — a two-handed shield held against the body for full-power kicks, knees and drive drills. Its size spreads the force of a hard kick across the holder instead of concentrating it, which is what makes it the right tool for power rounds, conditioning work and team drills in boxing, MMA and Muay Thai gyms.

T5 — COACHING MITTS

Coaching Mitts

Compact mitts built for one-to-one pad holding — personal training sessions, small-group coaching and technique work where the coach calls the shots and the fighter answers. Light, quick to reposition and easy on the coach's wrist across a full day of sessions, with the same layered foam build as the rest of the range.

T6 — CUSTOM & PRIVATE LABEL

Custom & Private Label Focus Pads

Any family above, produced as your product: your logo on the striking face or side, your panel colourways, your size grading, your packaging. Gyms building a house range and brands extending a fight-gear line both start here — send a reference pad or a tech pack and we pattern to it.

02 — INSIDE THE PAD

Built in Layers, Like It Should Be

A focus pad is a layered product, and each layer has a job — including protecting the coach who holds it. This is the build your order is manufactured to, and the explanation to ask any manufacturer for before you compare prices, because two pads that look alike in a photo can be built completely differently inside.

Outer ShellGenuine leather or synthetic — shaped panels that hold the pad's form and take the abrasion of daily pad work.
Foam SystemPadding built up in layers so impact is spread across the pad, with the striking face weighted for the blows the pad is made for — punches on mitts, kicks and knees on Thai pads.
Grip & StrapsA secure grip and strap system that keeps the pad in the coach's hand under hard work — a pad that twists or slips is a pad that is not working, and a risk to the holder.
StitchingPanels joined on industrial machines, with reinforcement at the points that fail first on cheap pads — the striking face edges, the grip and the strap anchors.

Pattern & Cutting

Every design starts from its own pattern set. Shell panels are marked and cut to the design, and the curve of a mitt, the length of a Thai pad and the grip shape all come off the pattern — which is why a manufacturer's pattern library matters: our catalogue carries twelve focus pad styles and six Thai pad styles, each with its own established pattern to manufacture against or adapt to your design.

Stitching & Reinforcement

Panels are joined on industrial sewing machines, with reinforcement at the points that fail first on cheap pads — striking face edges, grip and strap anchors. Stitching is checked pad by pad as production runs, alongside shape and strap function, before anything is packed.

One Honest Note About Protection

No focus pad — ours or anyone's — removes the force of a hard punch or kick; it spreads it, so the coach can hold pads session after session without the punishment landing on bare hands and arms. That is the pad's real job, and it is why the foam build and the grip matter more than the logo on the face. We would rather explain that plainly here, the way we do to buyers on WhatsApp, than win an order with a claim no manufacturer can honestly make.

How to Choose the Size

  1. Start from the training, not the pad: punches only means mitts; kicks and knees mean Thai pads or shields.
  2. Match the pad to the holder's hand and arm — a mitt that is too large for the coach twists on impact; a Thai pad that is too short leaves the holder's forearm exposed.
  3. For junior coaching, order junior mitts sized for smaller hands — adult mitts on a young coach's hand are a common cause of wrist strain.

What is the difference between focus mitts, Thai pads and kick shields?

Focus mitts are small, hand-held pads worn by the coach for punches — they give fast, precise feedback on combinations. Thai pads are larger, forearm-held pads built to absorb kicks, knees and elbows in Muay Thai and kickboxing. Kick shields are the biggest of the three, a two-handed shield held against the body for full-power kicks and knee drills. Many buyers order all three together as one pad-training range, which is why we manufacture them under one roof.

How are your focus pads padded and constructed?

In layers: a shaped outer shell, a foam system built up in layers so impact is spread across the pad instead of passing straight through to the coach's hand or arm, a grip and strap system that keeps the pad secure during hard work, and reinforced stitching at the points that take the most punishment — the striking face edges, the grip and the strap anchors.
