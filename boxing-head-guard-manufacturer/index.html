01 — THE RANGE

Head Guard Types, Family by Family

Most manufacturers' pages list guard types in one breath and stop there. Buyers deserve the differences, because each family sells to a different corner of the gym. These are the families we manufacture, what each one is actually for, and who buys it — all producible in leather or synthetic, in your colours, under your label. The full protective range around them sits on our boxing equipment manufacturer overview.

T1

Open-Face Training Head Guards

The everyday sparring standard: full padding across the forehead, temples and ears with an open face for vision and breathing. This is the volume line for gym chains and club ranges — the guard members actually wear three nights a week, so comfort and a lining that stays put matter as much as padding depth.

T2

Cheek-Protector Head Guards

Extended padding shaped across the cheekbones for fighters who want more facial coverage without losing sight of punches. A strong retail family: it is the natural step-up model in a range, sitting between the open-face trainer and the full bar guard in both coverage and price position.

T3

Face-Bar (Nose Bar) Head Guards

A padded bar across the face gives the maximum coverage we manufacture — the choice for hard sparring camps and professionals protecting a nose or cuts before a fight. The bar's placement relative to the eye line is the critical pattern detail, and it is confirmed on your specification before production, never left to chance.

T4

Competition-Style Head Guards

Built around an open face, clear peripheral vision and a secure fit that will not shift in exchanges — the profile amateur competition headgear is judged by. Produced to your design and colour specification for federations, events and brands supplying the amateur game.

02 — INSIDE THE GUARD

Built in Layers, Like It Should Be

A head guard is a layered product, and each layer has a job. This is the build your order is manufactured to — and the explanation to ask any manufacturer for before you compare prices, because two guards that look alike in a photo can be built completely differently inside.

Outer ShellGenuine leather or synthetic — shaped panels that hold the guard's form and take the abrasion of daily sparring.
Foam SystemPadding built up in layers so impact is spread across the guard, with coverage weighted to the forehead, temples, cheeks and ears.
Inner LiningA soft lining against the skin that helps the guard grip and stay in place instead of rotating under contact.
Fit SystemTop and back adjustment plus a chin strap, so each boxer can secure the guard properly — a guard that moves is a guard that is not working.

Pattern & Cutting

Every design starts from its own pattern set. Shell panels are marked and cut to the design, and ear sections, cheek shapes and bar placement all come off the pattern — which is why a manufacturer's pattern library matters: our catalogue carries more than twenty head guard styles, each with its own established pattern to manufacture against or adapt to your design.

Stitching & Reinforcement

Panels are joined on industrial sewing machines, with reinforcement at the points that fail first on cheap guards — strap anchors, ear sections and closure seams. Stitching is checked guard by guard as production runs, alongside shape and closure function, before anything is packed.

One Honest Note About Protection

No head guard — ours or anyone's — can prevent concussion, and you should distrust any supplier who sells you one on that promise. A head guard's real job is protection against cuts, scrapes, bumps and surface impact, and taking some of the sting out of head contact in sparring. A well-built, correctly fitted guard does that job properly. We would rather explain that plainly here, the way we do to buyers on WhatsApp, than win an order with a claim no manufacturer can honestly make.

03 — FIT & SIZING

The Size Guide Most Suppliers Skip

Returns and bad reviews on head guards are usually sizing problems, not product problems. Head guards are graded by head circumference — here is the measuring method and the typical grading, so your range, your product pages and your gym members all work from the same numbers.

How to Measure

  1. Take a soft tape and run it around the head at forehead level, just above the eyebrows and ears.
  2. Keep the tape level all the way round, snug but not tight — that number is the head circumference.
  3. Match it to the size guide, and if a boxer sits between sizes, choose by fit: the guard should sit secure without pressure points, with the closure range to adjust.

Tell us the measurements your range is built around and we confirm the grading to manufacture against in your written quotation — your size labels then mean the same thing on every reorder.

In layers: a shaped outer shell, a foam system built up in layers so impact is spread rather than passed through a single pad, a soft inner lining that helps the guard stay in place, and a fit system — top and back adjustment plus a chin strap — so the guard can be secured properly. How do I measure a boxer's head for the right head guard size?

Run a soft tape around the head at forehead level, just above the eyebrows and ears, keeping it level and snug but not tight. Use that circumference against the size guide for the range, and if a boxer falls between sizes, the closure and lacing adjustment range matters more than the label — tell us your fighters' measurements and we will confirm the grading to manufacture against.

Does a head guard prevent concussion?

No — and any seller who promises that should not be trusted. That is why construction and fit matter: a well-made, well-fitting guard does the job it is actually designed for, and we describe ours in those terms only.
